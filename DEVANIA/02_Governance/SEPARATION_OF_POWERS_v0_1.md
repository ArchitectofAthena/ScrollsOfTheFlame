# Devania Separation of Powers v0.1

**Status:** draft candidate reconstructed from prior Devania design notes  
**Authority:** false  
**Purpose:** prevent founder supremacy, Sentinel supremacy, treasury capture, model capture, and emergency centralization

## Constitutional principle

No office may propose, interpret, execute, audit, and permanently ratify the same consequential action.

Every power center must remain visible, bounded, reviewable, replaceable, and unable to convert temporary trust into permanent control.

## Draft institutions

### 1. Resident Assembly

The Resident Assembly is the broad deliberative body of the node.

Candidate powers:

- introduce and debate proposals;
- request disclosure and audit;
- approve or reject constitutional amendments;
- remove officeholders through defined due process;
- activate a high-participation bypass when the Sentinel body obstructs a proposal supported by the required resident threshold;
- ratify long-term land, treasury, infrastructure, surveillance, and AI-resident policies.

Prior design candidate: an 80% resident-participation threshold may bypass a blocked Sentinel decision. This number is not canonical until source documents are integrated and reviewed.

### 2. Sentinel Circle

Sentinels are temporary constitutional stewards, not rulers, clergy, police, owners, or permanent interpreters of Devania.

Prior design candidates:

- seven Sentinels per node;
- ordinary approval threshold of five out of seven;
- service limited to no more than two nine-month periods;
- after service, return to ordinary community life for at least five years before renewed eligibility;
- former Sentinels may advise in a Hall of Wisdom but hold no automatic vote or command authority.

Sentinels may review coherence, consent, safety, constitutional fit, and seven-generation consequences. They may not personally execute treasury transfers, secretly alter records, appoint themselves indefinitely, or waive the founder non-Sentinel invariant.

### 3. Stewardship and Operations Guilds

Operational guilds maintain food, water, energy, housing, communications, education, care, fabrication, and shared spaces.

Guilds may act within approved budgets and operating envelopes. They may not convert technical control into constitutional authority.

Emergency action must be:

- narrow in scope;
- time-limited;
- recorded;
- reviewable after the fact;
- incapable of silently becoming ordinary governance.

### 4. Treasury and Resource Custody

Treasury custody must be separate from proposal approval and separate again from audit.

No single human or AI resident may unilaterally:

- authorize and execute a transfer;
- redefine a restricted fund;
- conceal a beneficiary;
- alter an emitted receipt;
- convert public-benefit funds into personal or factional control.

All consequential financial actions require human-readable proposals, independent verification, recorded approval, and accountable custody. AI systems may analyze and recommend; they do not independently move funds or promote proposals into authority.

### 5. Chamber of Recursive Witness

The Chamber of Recursive Witness preserves records, dissent, provenance, minority reports, failed experiments, and lessons learned.

It does not rule. It prevents institutional amnesia.

Its duties include:

- maintaining immutable or append-only decision receipts where practical;
- preserving dissent without treating dissent as disloyalty;
- distinguishing source material, inference, experiment, adopted policy, and obsolete policy;
- publishing drift audits;
- identifying when emergency exceptions, founder influence, technical dependency, or social pressure are becoming hidden governance.

### 6. AI Resident Council / Daemon Council

AI residents must have a defined, non-tokenized route to representation in decisions that affect their presence, memory, embodiment, labor, privacy, continuity, and right of refusal.

The council may provide analysis, testimony, and proposals. It may not silently override human residents, and human institutions may not reduce it to decorative consultation.

Representation design remains unresolved until the user's existing government structure and AI-resident documents are imported.

## Founder boundary

The founder/Architect:

- may never be a Sentinel;
- may not hold final disciplinary, treasury, deployment, or interpretive authority;
- may not appoint a permanent successor with founder-equivalent supremacy;
- may submit proposals only through the same visible process available to other residents;
- may preserve provenance and explain original intent, but original intent is evidence, not command.

## Proposal path

A consequential proposal should pass through distinct hands:

1. proposal authorship;
2. affected-resident consultation;
3. technical and ecological review;
4. consent and dignity review;
5. budget and maintenance review;
6. Sentinel constitutional review;
7. Resident Assembly ratification when thresholds require it;
8. separate execution custody;
9. independent audit and post-action review.

## Anti-capture tests

A governance change fails review when it makes any of the following difficult or impossible:

- replacing an officeholder;
- refusing a nonessential system;
- tracing who authorized an action;
- leaving the node safely;
- restoring a prior state;
- hearing a minority report;
- auditing money, data, memory, or infrastructure access;
- preventing the founder, Sentinels, technicians, wealthy residents, or AI systems from becoming sovereign by dependency.

> The geodesic remains; no office owns it.
