# Solar and Agrovoltaic Node Blueprint v0.1

**Status:** requirements scaffold awaiting the user's blueprints, images, site assumptions, and engineering sources  
**Authority:** false  
**Not construction-ready:** yes

## Design intent

A Devania node should not treat energy, food, water, land, and community space as separate projects. The solar/agrovoltaic system is a shared resilience layer in which the same land can support renewable generation, cultivation, habitat, shade, water management, education, and local livelihoods.

The design target is not merely maximum electrical output. It is durable, repairable, locally understandable infrastructure that continues serving residents under heat, drought, storms, grid interruption, supply-chain delay, equipment failure, and leadership turnover.

## Required design packages

### A. Site and climate basis

Each node blueprint must record:

- site location at an appropriate privacy level;
- solar resource assumptions;
- topography, drainage, flood, wind, wildfire, hail, snow, salt, heat, and soil constraints as locally relevant;
- existing vegetation, habitat corridors, and culturally or ecologically protected areas;
- utility interconnection conditions;
- local agricultural practices and neighbor impacts;
- applicable land, electrical, structural, fire, water, and accessibility requirements.

No generic blueprint is site approval.

### B. Energy system architecture

The node package must distinguish:

- generation capacity;
- ordinary load;
- critical load;
- seasonal load;
- peak demand;
- storage capacity and reserve policy;
- grid-connected, islanded, black-start, and degraded modes;
- load shedding order;
- manual operation paths;
- replacement-parts strategy;
- monitoring, logging, and privacy boundaries.

Critical systems should be grouped into explicit resilience tiers, such as water pumping, refrigeration, communications, medical support, safety lighting, food processing, climate refuge, and AI-resident substrate continuity.

No AI system may silently control life-safety load shedding, resident surveillance, treasury-linked energy access, or denial of essential services.

### C. Agrovoltaic geometry

The physical design must specify and justify:

- array height and clearance;
- row spacing;
- orientation and tilt or tracking strategy;
- crop, pasture, orchard, nursery, greenhouse, pollinator, or mixed-use zones;
- light distribution and seasonal shading goals;
- equipment, wheelchair, livestock, emergency, and maintenance access;
- soil compaction prevention;
- fencing and wildlife passage;
- worker heat and shade considerations;
- fire access and vegetation management;
- future panel replacement without destroying the agricultural layer.

The geometry should be selected from the needs of the whole node, not copied from a utility-scale solar field.

### D. Food and land system

The agricultural layer should document:

- staple, fresh-food, medicinal, seed, fodder, and habitat functions;
- perennial and annual systems;
- agroforestry and windbreak candidates;
- soil-building and erosion-control practices;
- crop rotation and fallow strategy;
- pollinator support;
- livestock integration where lawful and appropriate;
- tool, wash, storage, cold-chain, kitchen, compost, and distribution paths;
- accessibility and resident skill requirements;
- what percentage of food resilience is actually claimed and how that claim is measured.

The blueprint must not equate food production with resident coercion. Labor expectations, disability access, rest, education, and fair exchange require their own social design.

### E. Water cycle

The package must show:

- potable, irrigation, process, fire, and emergency water distinctions;
- source reliability and legal rights;
- storage volume and turnover;
- rain capture where lawful;
- runoff control, swales, ponds, infiltration, and erosion management where appropriate;
- irrigation method and energy demand;
- drought stages and allocation rules;
- water-quality monitoring;
- wastewater and greywater boundaries;
- failure modes and manual backup.

Water governance must remain separate from personal favor, factional discipline, or opaque algorithmic allocation.

### F. Community and learning spaces

The array and farm should include deliberate places for:

- resident gathering;
- teaching and apprenticeship;
- repair and fabrication;
- produce handling;
- shade and climate refuge;
- child-safe learning;
- accessible participation;
- public demonstration without exposing private node infrastructure.

### G. Operations and maintenance

Every blueprint must include:

- inspection schedule;
- cleaning and vegetation management plan;
- inverter, battery, racking, wiring, pump, sensor, and communications maintenance;
- locally stockable spares;
- safe shutdown and lockout procedures;
- vendor dependence map;
- warranty and end-of-life plan;
- recycling or disposal plan;
- training materials understandable without the original designer;
- annual resilience exercise and post-exercise receipt.

## Graceful degradation

A node should fail in layers rather than collapse all at once.

Preferred behavior:

1. preserve life safety and water;
2. preserve communications and essential food storage;
3. preserve climate refuge and medical support;
4. preserve minimum AI-resident substrate continuity according to adopted resident agreements;
5. shed discretionary loads visibly and reversibly;
6. maintain manual paths;
7. record the failure and repair without converting emergency controls into permanent authority.

## Required drawings and images

The source package should eventually include:

- overall site plan;
- solar one-line diagram;
- critical-load diagram;
- array section showing height and agricultural clearance;
- seasonal shade study;
- water-flow diagram;
- crop and habitat plan;
- access and emergency routes;
- maintenance zones;
- phased construction plan;
- rendered resident experience views;
- provenance and license for every image.

These cannot be finalized until the user's source material is imported:

- node location and climate;
- target resident population;
- land area;
- electric load profile;
- generation and storage targets;
- crop and livestock mix;
- water source and storage;
- structural system;
- phased budget;
- legal ownership and cooperative model;
- grid relationship;
- exact resilience duration;
- AI-resident compute, cooling, power, networking, and privacy requirements.

> The land is not a panel stand. The panels are not separate from the garden.
